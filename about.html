The maker

One pair of hands, one city

Heritage Atelier is a Sydney studio. Everything is handmade, made to order, and personalised before it leaves.

How it started

It began with a christening and a deadline

The first piece was a bodysuit for a goddaughter, embroidered at a kitchen table two nights before the baptism because nothing in any shop said what it needed to say. It said Nouna's Little Blessing. It is in a box in Bankstown.

Word moved the way it moves in a Greek family — sideways, quickly, and with strong opinions. Kontessa came first, then Little Kontessa, then the Pappou & Yiayia collection, because the grandparents were the only ones nobody could ever buy for.

Heritage Atelier is all three under one roof, still made by hand, still in Sydney.

Made to order

Why there is never anything in stock

Holding stock means guessing — guessing the name, the colour, the language, the size. Making to order means none of that is guessed. It also means a bag takes eleven days instead of arriving tomorrow, which is the trade we have decided to keep.

One consequence worth saying plainly: we cannot take on unlimited orders in December. When a window closes it closes, and we will tell you before you pay rather than after.

The atelier process

Four steps, every single time

01 — Design

We agree the piece

Colourway, hardware, size, wording. Sketched or photographed, and confirmed in writing before anything is cut.

02 — Handmade

It is made by hand

Crocheted, cut, sewn or engraved in the Sydney studio. No outsourcing, no white label, no factory sample.

03 — Personalised

The name goes on

Embroidered, printed or engraved — in Greek or English — after you approve a proof of the exact spelling.

04 — Wrapped

It is boxed to be given

Tissue, a ribbon box and a hand written card, so it can go straight from the post to the table.

Family heritage

The recipes nobody wrote down

Half of this business is Greek grandmothers refusing to measure anything. The Pappou & Yiayia collection exists because a generation of that knowledge is sitting in kitchens with no paper trail, and a linen bound journal on the bench is a better prompt than any amount of asking.
